Play Skyscrapers (Towers) online Works — plays right here, unlimited puzzles

Guide updated October 2026 · Runs via WebAssembly (native port)

Skyscrapers is a Latin-square puzzle shown as a city of towers: each row and column has one tower of every height, and the numbers round the edge say how many towers you would see looking in from that side, with tall towers hiding shorter ones behind them. How to play

On an N×N grid, build a tower in each square with a height from 1 to N so that:

  • each height appears once in every row and once in every column;
  • each edge clue equals the number of towers visible from that point, looking along the row or column; a tower is hidden if any taller tower stands in front of it.

So a clue of N means the heights rise steadily away from it, and a clue of 1 means the tallest tower is right next to it. In larger or harder puzzles some towers are placed for you and some edge clues are missing.

Sizes and difficulty

Presets cover 4×4 Easy, 5×5 Easy and Hard, and 6×6 at Easy, Hard, Extreme and Unreasonable. Unreasonable needs some trial and error; the other levels can be reasoned out. Custom goes from 3×3 up to 9×9. If you find the 3D drawing hard to read, Preferences can switch to a flat 2D view with plain numbers.

Solving tips

Place the certainties first: a clue of 1 puts the tallest tower beside it, and a clue of N fixes the whole line in rising order. A tower of height N can never be closer to a clue than (clue − 1) squares away, so a clue of 3 keeps the tallest tower out of the first two squares. Opposite clues that add up to N+1 often pin the tallest tower's position exactly.

Where this puzzle comes from

Towers is part of Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection, a set of about forty logic puzzles written by Simon Tatham and contributors and released under the MIT licence. Every grid is generated on the spot, so the supply never runs out, and for the grid-logic puzzles the generator makes sure each has exactly one solution, reachable by reasoning rather than guessing. The version here is the collection's own code compiled to WebAssembly; the Game menu can save a puzzle to a file and load it again, and the Type menu changes the size and difficulty.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to count the tower right next to the clue?

Yes. The nearest tower is always visible, so every clue is at least 1.

Is there one correct answer?

Yes. Every difficulty level, including Unreasonable, is generated with a single solution.

Is Skyscrapers free to play here?

Yes, with no limit on the number of puzzles. Towers is open-source software under the MIT licence, and every grid is generated fresh when you press New game.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. A tap works as a click and the puzzle resizes to fit the screen. Puzzles whose controls use the right mouse button are easier with a mouse.
