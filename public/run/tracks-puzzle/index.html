Play Train tracks puzzle (Tracks) online Works — plays right here, unlimited puzzles

Guide updated October 2026 · Runs via WebAssembly (native port)

Tracks is a railway logic puzzle. A train line must run from A on one edge of the grid to B on another, using only straight pieces and right-angle bends. Numbers along the top and right say how many squares in each column and row hold track, and a few pieces are already laid. The puzzle is ready below; click between squares to lay rail.

How to play

  • Lay one continuous track from A to B.
  • Track pieces are straight or 90-degree curves; the track may not cross or branch.
  • Each clue gives how many squares in that column (top) or row (right) contain track.
  • Pieces already shown at the start are part of the answer.

Sizes and difficulty

The Type menu runs from 8x8 to 15x15, at Easy, Tricky and, for the 10x10 and 15x15 grids, Hard. Tricky and Hard need you to rule out moves that would force an impossible crossing later. By default the generator avoids neighbouring rows or columns both having a clue of 1; Custom lets you turn that restriction off.

Solving tips

Rows and columns with a clue of 0 are empty; mark them with crosses straight away. Count against the clue: when a row already has as many track squares as its number, cross out the rest. A square with track must have exactly two connections, so if three of its sides are blocked the path is forced. Look at A and B first, since the line must leave each one in a fixed direction.

Where this puzzle comes from

Tracks is part of Simon Tatham's Portable Puzzle Collection, a set of about forty logic puzzles written by Simon Tatham and contributors and released under the MIT licence. Every grid is generated on the spot, so the supply never runs out, and for the grid-logic puzzles the generator makes sure each has exactly one solution, reachable by reasoning rather than guessing. The version here is the collection's own code compiled to WebAssembly; the Game menu can save a puzzle to a file and load it again, and the Type menu changes the size and difficulty.

Frequently asked questions

Can the track visit a square twice or cross itself?

No. Each square holds at most one piece, either straight or curved, and the line never crosses or branches.

What do the shaded squares mean?

They are your own notes: a left-click inside a square marks it as definitely containing track before you know which way the track runs through it.

Is Tracks free to play here?

Yes, with no limit on the number of puzzles. Tracks is open-source software under the MIT licence, and every grid is generated fresh when you press New game.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. A tap works as a click and the puzzle resizes to fit the screen. Puzzles whose controls use the right mouse button are easier with a mouse.
